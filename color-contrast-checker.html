Free WCAG Color Contrast Checker

Color contrast is one of the most important aspects of accessible web design. The Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) define minimum contrast ratios to ensure text is readable for everyone, including the estimated 300 million people worldwide with color vision deficiencies. A contrast checker measures the luminance difference between two colors — typically your text color and background color — and tells you whether the combination meets accessibility standards. Low contrast doesn't just affect people with disabilities; it impacts everyone reading on bright screens, in sunlight, or on low-quality displays.

Understanding WCAG Contrast Standards

WCAG 2.1 defines two conformance levels for contrast. Level AA is the baseline requirement for most websites and applications: a minimum contrast ratio of 4.5:1 for normal text and 3:1 for large text (18pt or 14pt bold and above). Level AAA is the enhanced standard: 7:1 for normal text and 4.5:1 for large text. Most legal accessibility requirements (ADA, Section 508, EN 301 549) reference AA compliance. AAA is recommended for body text, long-form reading, and critical UI elements like form labels and error messages.

How Contrast Ratio Is Calculated

The contrast ratio formula uses relative luminance, a measure of the perceived brightness of a color. For each color, the RGB values are normalized to 0-1 and gamma-corrected: R' = (R/255)^2.2, and similarly for G and B. Relative luminance is then L = 0.2126 × R' + 0.7152 × G' + 0.0722 × B'. The contrast ratio between two colors is (L1 + 0.05) / (L2 + 0.05), where L1 is the lighter color's luminance. The result ranges from 1:1 (no contrast, identical colors) to 21:1 (maximum contrast, black on white).

How to Fix Low Contrast

If your color combination fails WCAG requirements, you have several options. The simplest fix is to darken your text or lighten your background. For colored text on colored backgrounds, adjust one color's lightness in HSL until the ratio passes. Avoid common mistakes: light gray text on white (#999 on #FFF is only 2.8:1), colored text on colored backgrounds without checking, and relying on color alone to convey information. Placeholder text in form inputs is a frequent offender — ensure it meets at least 4.5:1 for AA compliance.

Common Contrast Mistakes

Many websites fail contrast requirements in predictable ways. Light gray body text (#777 on #FFF = 4.5:1, barely passing) is common but becomes unreadable on mobile screens. Colored links on colored backgrounds often fail — blue links (#0066CC) on a dark navy background may look fine to some users but fail for others. Disabled button states are frequently too low-contrast to read. Footer text in small, light gray type is another common issue. Testing with a contrast checker during design — not after development — prevents costly rework and ensures every user can read your content.

Frequently Asked Questions
What is WCAG contrast?

WCAG (Web Content Accessibility Guidelines) contrast requirements ensure text is readable for people with visual impairments. The guidelines define minimum contrast ratios between text color and background color, measured as a ratio like 4.5:1. Higher ratios mean better readability. WCAG is maintained by the W3C and referenced by accessibility laws worldwide.

What ratio do I need for AA?

WCAG AA requires a contrast ratio of at least 4.5:1 for normal text (under 18pt regular or 14pt bold) and at least 3:1 for large text (18pt+ regular or 14pt+ bold). AA is the most commonly required compliance level and satisfies most legal accessibility requirements including ADA and Section 508.

What ratio for AAA?

WCAG AAA requires a contrast ratio of at least 7:1 for normal text and at least 4.5:1 for large text. AAA is the highest level of contrast compliance and is recommended for body text, long-form reading, and critical interface elements like form labels and error messages.

How do I improve contrast?

To improve contrast, darken your text color or lighten your background color (or vice versa for light-on-dark designs). In HSL terms, increase the lightness difference between your two colors. Avoid light gray text on white backgrounds. Use our contrast checker to test combinations until you meet your target AA or AAA threshold. Small adjustments of 10-15% in lightness often make the difference.
